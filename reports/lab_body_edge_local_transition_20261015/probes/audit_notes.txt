Independent image-only audit: 2.png U2 / U4 / U5 upper contours

Source: lab_body_edge_short_20261015/originals/2.png and measurements.json.
No laboratory measurement values were read, fitted or used in this audit.
Images are original pixel crops; their nearest-neighbor enlargement is only for display.

Finding: The U2 cyan line lies on the opening background beyond the dark lip.
At x=1725,1763,1801 its y coordinates are 1621.09,1620.24,1619.40.
The small-kernel local outer-lip gradient peaks are at y=1616,1617,1616.
The line is therefore 3.2 to 5.1 pixels openingward of the visible transition
at these three representative columns. These are image-space observations,
not metrological truth or hand-annotated point coordinates.

Cause: The first residual-threshold departure from an extrapolated background
is an onset locator, not the transition center. It may be displaced by Gaussian
blur tails, background slope, threshold, or local lip contrast. Narrowing the
horizontal fit span does not fix this vertical boundary definition.

The outer dark-lip transition has positive dI/dy in U2 and U4. The much stronger
negative gradient farther inside the metal is the bright-face transition and
must not be substituted for the selected physical contour. U5's black gap has
no independently visible reversed-polarity lip; its outermost substantial
negative-gradient event is the useful visible contour.

Critical counterexample: U4 x=1175 has an old onset y=1614.83, with positive
departure polarity toward the metal. This onset already missed the small dark
lip. The actual outer-lip positive gradient peak is y=1618 (6.34 gray levels/px),
while stronger negative bright-face gradients are farther inside. Refining only
inward from the old onset with its old polarity will choose the wrong event.
U4 other sampled columns have outer peaks at y=1619. A uniform upward shift
or blindly preserving every onset polarity is therefore not justified.

Suggested detector behavior:
1. Keep background departure / coherent bright-face branch only as coarse locators.
2. In a bounded neighborhood, identify locally significant transition events
   from actual lightly smoothed image profiles, allowing either polarity.
3. Prefer the opening-facing event with spatial support across the side anchors;
   do not let the globally strongest bright-face event steal a gray-lip contour.
4. Locate the chosen event by its derivative peak or a local step fit, with
   subpixel interpolation. Stop at the event's derivative reversal/valley before
   any separate interior event. Save onset and refined point separately.
5. If the outer event is not resolvable, retain an ambiguity flag or blank result;
   do not invent a fixed pixel translation or force a horizontal line.
